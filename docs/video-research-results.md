## How the numbers were made

- **Pool.** Every candidate id from `docs/video-research.md`, plus 4 to 6 API searches per lesson (12 results each, English, relevance order with a publish-date floor). That produced 31 to 69 raw hits per lesson.
- **Drops.** Anything over 20:00. Anything under 2:00 (Shorts). Anything with Cowork in the title or description. Product lessons (0.2, 2.4, 3.1, 4.1, 4.3, 5.1, 5.3): anything published before 2026. Concept lessons (1.1, 2.1, 5.2): kept pre-2026 videos that are about ideas, not UI. Also dropped: off-topic search noise, non-English titles, and terminal-first Claude Code. The survivors are 8 to 12 per lesson.
- **Comments.** The top 30 comment threads by relevance for each survivor, with the creator's own comments removed. The tally is keyword-based. Praise means clear, concise, helpful, to the point, easy to follow, thanks and similar. Complaints mean clickbait, fluff, outdated, misleading, too fast, waste of time, sponsor and similar. Every comment section was also read by eye. The quotes are verbatim, trimmed with "…".
- **Score.** Meat per minute, 1 to 10. How much of the runtime likely teaches this lesson's outcome to a beginner, judged from title, chapters and description plus what commenters say they learned. This is not a transcript score.

## What could not be verified

- **Transcripts.** The API does not return them, so no video was watched or read. Scores come from titles, chapters, descriptions and comments. Every finalist still needs the transcript review in step 3 of the plan.
- **On-screen UI.** The data can't tell whether a video shows the current app. Dates and comments are the only signal, and the late-October rebuild makes this the biggest open risk.
- **Spoken language and voice.** Some videos may be in another language (`rgbnMGOV9cY` has mostly Urdu/Hindi comments). Some may be AI-voiced or AI-avatar (`WdZjoMi6W7Y`, `X5qdQ0PquD8`, `Mmo-xKT3C5g` per commenters). None of this can be confirmed without watching.
- **Comment tallies.** The tallies are keyword matches, read by eye but not hand-coded. Relevance order is YouTube's. Several sections carry obvious spam (repeated "Trent Calloway" and "teamorouter" plugs), and the regex counted some of it as praise. Where it skewed a tally, that is noted in the score.
- **Likes.** Some channels hide likes (shown as —). `GoApGBB2S_E` has an abnormal like ratio that could be bought engagement. It can't be proven.
- **Sponsorships and funnels.** These were inferred from descriptions only. In-video ad reads are invisible to the API.
- **Videos with zero to five comments.** About a third of survivors have no meaningful comment evidence. They score low for lack of signal, not because they were judged bad.
