## Brief

A good prompt has four parts: **goal** (the outcome you want), **context** (what Claude can't know), **format** (the shape of the answer) and **example** (what good looks like). When the output is bad, one of them is usually missing. Test any prompt by asking: could a smart stranger act on this?

## Task

The bad prompt rewrite. Feel the difference.

1. **Run the bad prompt.** New chat, paste this exactly, and read what comes back:
```
Write an email to my client about the delay.
```
2. **Write a better prompt** for the same outcome, in a new chat, using all four parts. Invent a realistic situation if you need to: who the client is, what the delay is and why, what you want them to do next, how you normally sound. Paste a real email of yours as the example if you have one.
3. **Compare the two outputs side by side.** Which could you actually send? Which piece of context made the biggest difference?
4. **Write that answer in your notes below.** One sentence.

> If the better prompt still produced something you wouldn't send, find the thin part, add to it, and run it again.

## Takeaways

- Goal, context, format, example. When output is bad, one is missing.
- Could a smart stranger act on your message? If not, neither can Claude.
