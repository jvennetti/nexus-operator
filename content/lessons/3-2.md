## Brief

Email and calendar are where information arrives. Your work tools are where it gets done: the task manager, the docs, the shared drive, the team chat. Connect those and Claude stops being a place you go and starts being a colleague who's in the same tools you are.

Look through the Connectors list for the tools you actually use day to day. Common ones: ClickUp, Notion, Asana, Google Drive, Slack. Connect the one or two you live in. Don't connect everything. More connectors means more for Claude to consider and more permissions to think about. Start with what you touch daily.

What this makes possible:

- **Email becomes tasks.** "Read the thread from the client and create tasks in ClickUp for everything we promised, with due dates."
- **Docs become answers.** "Find our onboarding doc in Drive and tell me what we say about the first week."
- **Chat becomes summary.** "What did the team decide in the project channel this week?"
- **Updates write themselves.** "Look at my open tasks and draft a status update for the team."

The pattern is always: information lives in one tool, needs to end up in another, and a human used to carry it across by hand. Now you say the sentence and it's carried.

## Task

Connect your main work tool and carry something across.

1. **Connect one or two work tools** you use every day in Settings → Connectors.
2. **Ask Claude what it can see.** "What can you access in [tool]? Give me a quick overview of what's in there." Confirm it's seeing what you expect.
3. **Carry something across.** Take an email thread or a set of meeting notes and say: "Create tasks in [tool] for everything that needs doing here. Assign to me, due this week, and show me the list before you create them." Review, approve, done.
4. **Pull something out.** "Give me a status summary of everything I have open in [tool], grouped by project, in order of urgency."
5. **Check your work.** Open the tool itself. Confirm the tasks are really there and look right. Fix anything off by telling Claude.

## Takeaways

- Connect the one or two work tools you actually live in. Not everything.
- The core move: information in one tool, needed in another, carried across by a sentence instead of by hand.
- Ask Claude to show you what it's about to create before it creates it. Review, then approve.
