## Brief

By now you've noticed something annoying: every new chat starts from zero. You explain who you are, who the client is, how you like things written, and then next week you explain it all again.

**Projects** fix that. A Project is a folder in the sidebar that holds two things:

- **Instructions.** Standing guidance that applies to every chat inside the Project. Who you are. What this project is about. How you want things written. What to always do and never do.
- **Knowledge.** Files Claude can see in every conversation inside the Project. Your brand voice doc. The client brief. The product spec. Your past proposals. The template you always use.

Start a chat inside the Project and Claude already knows all of it. You skip straight to the work.

When to make a Project: any time you'll have more than three conversations about the same thing. A client. A side project. A recurring responsibility. Your job itself. Many operators run a "Me" project whose instructions describe who they are and how they work, and use it for anything personal.

What goes in instructions vs. a single chat: instructions are for things that are true every time. One-off material (today's email, this week's numbers) goes into the chat where it's needed, not into the Project.

## Challenge

Build one real Project you'll keep using.

1. **Pick one area** of your work or life that comes up constantly. A client. A team. A recurring responsibility. If nothing jumps out, make it your job.
2. **Write the instructions with Claude's help.** New chat, paste this:
```
I'm creating a Claude Project for [the area]. Interview me, one question at a time, until you can write a set of Project instructions that covers: who I am in this context, what the project is about, who the usual audience is, how I like things written, and three things you should always do and two you should never do. Then write the instructions.
```
   Answer the questions. Edit the result until it sounds like you.
3. **Create the Project** in the sidebar. Paste the instructions in.
4. **Add two to four files** to the Project's knowledge. The documents you'd otherwise attach to every chat about this: a brief, a template, an example of good output, a reference doc.
5. **Run one real task inside it** without explaining any of the background. Notice how much shorter your prompt is.

## Takeaways

- A Project holds instructions and files that every chat inside it can see. Set up once, used forever.
- Make a Project for anything you'll discuss more than three times. Clients, responsibilities, your job itself.
- Instructions are for what's true every time. One-off material goes in the individual chat.
