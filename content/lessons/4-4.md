## Brief

Time to build something. This video shows the order that works in the Code tab: Plan mode, Claude's questions, a plan with reasons, then Revise until it's right, and only then Accept. He builds a website. You'll build a tool you'll use.

## Challenge

Build a tool you'll use.

1. Pick a task from your list that involves files: a report you compile, a folder you tidy, a spreadsheet you fill in.
2. In the **Code** tab, on nexus-workspace, switch the mode selector to **Plan**. Describe the thing in one plain sentence, then add:
```
Ask me what you need to know first.
```
3. Answer its questions. Read the plan and the reasons. Press **Revise** at least once with a real cut: "Drop X, I'll never use it." Accept only when you'd sign it.
4. Switch back to **Manual** and let it build. Say yes to creating new files.
5. Open the result from Finder or Explorer, not just the preview. Give three rounds of notes. Then break it on purpose (give it bad input) and say "fix it."

Mark this done when you've used the thing on real work once.

## Takeaways

- Plan first. Reading a plan is cheaper than reading a mistake.
- Revise is the most useful button. Cut what you don't need before it's built.
- Three rounds of notes is normal. The first version is never the last.
- Suggestion: a page, a spreadsheet tool, a tidy-up script. Small and used beats big and abandoned.
