## Brief

Now you build something useful that didn't exist yesterday and that you can open tomorrow. It doesn't have to be software. First-week examples:

- A dashboard page of this week's priorities, pulled from a notes file.
- A tool that turns a folder of receipts into an expense spreadsheet.
- A script that sorts a messy downloads folder.

Describe it like any build: what it is, who it's for, what it does, how it should feel. Ask for it in steps: a plan first, then the simplest version, then improvements.

When something breaks, paste the error and say "fix it." You don't need to understand why.

## Task

Build one useful thing for yourself.

1. **Choose something small** from your Operator List, or one of the examples above.
2. **Ask for a plan first.** "I want to build [the thing]. It's for [who], and it needs to [what]. Before building anything, give me a short plan: what files you'll create and what the simplest working version looks like."
3. **Build the simplest version** and use it for real, even if it's rough.
4. **Give three rounds of notes:** what's wrong, what's missing, what should look different.
5. **Break it and fix it.** Try something it wasn't built for. When it fails, paste the error or describe what happened and say "fix it."

## Takeaways

- Plan, then the simplest version, then improve in rounds. Small steps are easy to steer.
- When it breaks, paste the error and say "fix it."
