## Brief

We saved this for now because it only makes sense once you've felt it. By this point you have: a long chat where Claude started forgetting what you said at the top, a Claude Code session that got slower and weirder the longer it ran. That's not random. There's a mechanical reason, and once you know it, you'll stop fighting it.

Every message you send, Claude re-reads the entire conversation from the beginning. Not just your new message, everything. That's how it stays coherent. But there's a limit to how much it can hold at once, and as a conversation grows, the early parts get crowded out. Instructions from the top get less weight. Details blur. Output quality drops.

This is called the **context window**, and managing it is a real skill. The good news is that you already have all the tools. You've been collecting them since Module 2:

- **Start fresh often.** New task, new chat or session. It feels wasteful. It's the opposite.
- **Put standing context in files, not in the conversation.** Project instructions. `CLAUDE.md`. Skills. Reference docs. Those are read every time without taking up conversation space the way a long thread does.
- **Hand off, don't continue.** When a conversation is long and you're not done, ask for a summary of where things stand, then start fresh with that summary. Five lines of summary beat fifty messages of history.
- **Don't paste everything.** Paste what's relevant. If Claude needs a 40-page document, put it in the Project or the folder and let Claude read the part it needs.
- **Watch for the signs.** Repeating itself, forgetting a rule you set, getting vaguer. That's the window filling. Hand off and restart.

Once you work this way, long tasks stop degrading. Each session starts sharp, reads its context from files, does its piece, writes down where it got to.

## Challenge

Rescue a long conversation and feel the difference.

1. **Find a long chat or session** from earlier in this course. One where you'd been going back and forth for a while.
2. **Ask for the handoff.** At the end of it: "Summarize where we are: what we've decided, what's done, what's left, and any rules or preferences I've given you in this conversation. Keep it under 15 lines."
3. **Start fresh.** New chat (or new session in the same folder). Paste the summary and say "continue from here."
4. **Compare.** Give the new conversation the next step of the task. Notice: faster, sharper, remembers the rules.
5. **Move the standing stuff out.** Look at the summary. Anything in it that's a permanent rule or preference belongs in Project instructions or `CLAUDE.md`, not in a conversation. Move it now.

## Takeaways

- Claude re-reads the whole conversation every message. Long conversations crowd out early instructions and degrade output.
- Start fresh often. Standing context belongs in files (Projects, `CLAUDE.md`, skills), not in the thread.
- When a conversation is long and unfinished, ask for a short handoff summary and continue in a new one.
- Signs the window is full: repeating itself, forgetting rules, getting vaguer. Hand off and restart.
