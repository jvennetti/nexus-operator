## Brief

Working with Claude is managing. A good manager doesn't say "handle the client thing." They give the outcome, the context and what good looks like, then review the draft and send notes.

Three habits:

- **Be specific about the outcome.** "Write an email" is a wish. "Two paragraphs telling the client delivery slips to the 14th, and offering a call" is a brief.
- **Give the context it can't guess.** Paste the thread. Attach the document.
- **Iterate.** The first draft is a starting point. "Shorter." "More direct."

And when Claude hedges or says it can't do something: usually it can. Tell it you want it done anyway.

## Task

Take one task off your Operator List and get it done, start to finish.

1. **Pick the easiest one,** the one Claude said it could do today.
2. **Write a proper brief** in a new chat: the outcome, who it's for, the context, what good looks like. Attach or paste anything relevant.
3. **Send at least two rounds of notes.** Be blunt: "too long," "wrong tone," "you missed the deadline."
4. **If it hedges, push through.** Say: "I understand. I still want you to do it. Here's what you need."
5. **Use the output for real.** Send the email, file the document. The task is done when the real-world thing is done.

## Takeaways

- Brief Claude like a good manager: outcome, context, audience, what good looks like. Then give notes.
- When Claude hedges or says it can't, tell it you want it done anyway. It almost always can.
