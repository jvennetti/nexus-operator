## Brief

Think about what a good manager does when handing off work. They don't say "handle the client thing." They say who the client is, what happened, what outcome they want, when it's due, and what a good result looks like. Then they review the draft and send it back with notes.

That's it. That's the entire skill of working with Claude, and most people skip every step of it.

Three habits separate operators from everyone else:

- **Be specific about the outcome.** "Write an email" is a wish. "Write a two-paragraph email that tells the client the delivery slips to the 14th, takes responsibility without groveling, and offers a call" is a brief.
- **Give the context it can't guess.** Who, what, why, for whom, what's happened so far. Paste the thread. Attach the document. Claude can't read your mind, but it can read anything you give it.
- **Iterate.** The first draft is a starting point. "Shorter." "More direct." "That second line sounds like a robot." "Do it again but from their point of view." Every round is seconds, and every round gets closer.

One more thing you need to hear before we go further: **Claude will sometimes say it can't do something, or hedge, or give you a watered-down version.** Usually it can. Tell it plainly that you want it done, give it what it needs, and it will almost always deliver. Being direct is not rude. It's the job.

## Task

Take one task off your Operator List and get it actually done, start to finish.

1. **Pick the easiest one** from your Operator List. The one Claude said it could do today.
2. **Write a proper brief.** Open a new chat. Before you type, answer these in your head: What's the outcome? Who's it for? What context does Claude need? What does good look like? Then write the message. Attach or paste anything relevant.
3. **Review the draft like a manager.** Don't accept the first version. Send at least two rounds of notes. Be blunt: "too long," "wrong tone," "you missed the part about the deadline."
4. **If it pushes back or hedges, push through.** Say: "I understand. I still want you to do it. Here's what you need." Watch what happens.
5. **Use the output.** Actually send the email, file the document, use the plan. The task is only done when the real-world thing is done.

Mark the task done when you've used the result in real life.

## Takeaways

- Brief Claude the way a good manager briefs a person: outcome, context, audience, what good looks like.
- The first draft is a starting point. Reviewing and sending back notes is the normal workflow, not a sign something went wrong.
- When Claude hedges or says it can't, tell it plainly you want it done and give it what it needs. It almost always can.
