## Brief

Context does the most work in a prompt, and it's the part people shortchange. Give Claude three things:

- **The material itself.** Don't describe the thread, paste it. Attach the document, spreadsheet or screenshot. Claude reads them all.
- **What good looks like.** Attach a proposal you loved, or paste three emails in your voice. Claude matches an example far more closely than a description.
- **Who it's for.** The same facts written for your boss and for your client are two different pieces of writing.

## Task

Get something real done with real material.

1. **Find something messy from your work or life:** an email thread to answer, a long document, a spreadsheet, meeting notes.
2. **Give it to Claude whole,** untidied, in a new chat. Add who you are in this situation, who the output is for, and an example of good if you have one.
3. **Ask for the specific output:** a reply, a one-page summary, a list of actions, a table.
4. **Use it.** Send the reply, share the summary, act on the list.

## Takeaways

- Show, don't describe. Paste the thread, attach the file.
- An example of good beats a description. Say who it's for.
