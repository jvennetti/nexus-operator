## Brief

Of the four parts of a prompt, context is the one that does the heavy lifting, and it's the one people shortchange most. They describe the situation when they could just show it.

Claude can read almost anything you give it:

- **Documents.** Word files, PDFs, text, slides. Drag them in.
- **Spreadsheets.** Attach the file and ask questions about the numbers.
- **Screenshots.** A photo of a whiteboard, an error message, a chart, a page you can't copy. Claude reads images.
- **Pasted text.** The whole email thread. The meeting transcript. The messy notes. Don't summarize it for Claude. Paste it and let Claude do the summarizing.

The rule: **show, don't describe.** Describing takes effort and loses detail. Showing takes a drag-and-drop and loses nothing.

The second half of context is **what good looks like.** If you want a proposal, attach a proposal you loved. If you want your voice, paste three emails you wrote. If you want a particular structure, paste something with that structure. Claude will match it far more precisely than it could follow a description.

The third half (there are three halves) is **who it's for.** The same information written for your boss, your client and your mom is three different pieces of writing. Say who's reading.

## Task

Get something real done using real material.

1. **Find something messy from your actual work or life.** An email thread that needs a reply. A long document you need to understand. A spreadsheet you need to make sense of. Notes from a meeting that need to become actions. Something you'd otherwise put off.
2. **Give it to Claude whole.** New chat. Attach the file or paste the whole thing. Don't tidy it first.
3. **Add the three pieces of context:** who you are in this situation, who the output is for, and what good looks like (attach or paste an example if you have one).
4. **Ask for the specific output** you need. A reply. A one-page summary. A list of actions with owners. A table.
5. **Use it.** Send the reply, share the summary, act on the list. Real material, real output, real use.

Mark the task done when you've used the result.

## Takeaways

- Show, don't describe. Paste the whole thread, attach the file, drop in the screenshot.
- Attach an example of what good looks like. Claude matches examples far more precisely than descriptions.
- Say who it's for. The same facts written for different readers are different pieces of writing.
