## Brief

The single biggest difference between people who get great results and people who don't is what they do after the first answer. Most people read it, judge it, and either use it or give up. Operators treat the first answer as a draft and start a conversation.

A few moves that change everything:

- **Give notes, not retries.** Don't rewrite your prompt from scratch. Say what's wrong: "too formal," "cut the second paragraph," "you buried the ask, lead with it." Claude keeps everything that was right.
- **Make Claude interview you.** Add this to any prompt where you're not sure you've given enough: "Before you start, ask me any questions you need answered to do this well." Then answer them. The output quality jumps.
- **Ask for options.** "Give me three versions with different tones." Pick, combine, refine.
- **Ask it to critique itself.** "Read that back as the client. What would make you say no?" Then: "Fix those."
- **Push through the hedge.** Sometimes Claude will say it can't, or shouldn't, or will give you a careful half-version. Say: "I understand the caveats. I want you to do it fully. Here's the context you need." In almost every case, it does. Being direct is the job. You're the director.

None of these are tricks. They're what you'd do with a person. The only reason people don't do them with Claude is that it feels strange to send a computer notes. Get over that today.

## Challenge

Three rounds on one piece of work, plus one push.

1. **Start something that matters.** A message you need to send, a plan you need to make, a document you need to write. Give Claude a good brief (you know how now), and add: "Before you start, ask me whatever you need to know to do this well." Answer its questions.
2. **Round one: notes.** Read the draft. Give at least three specific notes in one message. Not "make it better." Specific.
3. **Round two: critique.** Say: "Read this back as [the person receiving it]. What's weak? What would make them hesitate?" Then: "Fix all of that."
4. **Round three: options.** Say: "Give me two alternative versions: one shorter and more direct, one warmer." Pick the best pieces of each and ask for a final.
5. **The push.** At some point in this course Claude will hedge or say it can't do something. Next time it happens, don't accept it. Say "I want you to do it anyway, fully. Here's what you need." Note in the box below what happened.

## Takeaways

- The first answer is a draft. Giving notes is the workflow.
- Add "ask me questions before you start" to any brief you're unsure about. The quality jump is large.
- Ask Claude to critique its own work from the reader's point of view, then fix it.
- When Claude hedges or says it can't, tell it plainly you want it done fully. It almost always can.
