## Brief

What you do after the first answer matters more than the first prompt. Treat it as a draft.

- **Give notes, not retries.** Say what's wrong ("too formal," "lead with the ask") instead of rewriting your prompt.
- **Make it interview you.** Add: "Before you start, ask me what you need to know to do this well."
- **Make it critique itself.** "Read this as the client. What would make you say no? Fix that."
- **Push through the hedge.** If Claude says it can't, or gives you a careful half-version, say: "I want you to do it anyway, fully. Here's what you need." It almost always can.

## Task

Three rounds on one piece of work, plus one push.

1. **Start something that matters** with a good brief, and add: "Before you start, ask me whatever you need to know to do this well." Answer its questions.
2. **Notes.** Give at least three specific notes in one message.
3. **Critique.** "Read this back as [the person receiving it]. What's weak? What would make them hesitate?" Then: "Fix all of that."
4. **Options.** "Give me two alternative versions: one shorter and more direct, one warmer." Combine the best parts into a final.
5. **The push.** The next time Claude hedges or says it can't, say: "I want you to do it anyway, fully. Here's what you need." Note what happened in the box below.

## Takeaways

- The first answer is a draft. Giving notes is the workflow.
- When Claude hedges or says it can't, tell it you want it done anyway.
