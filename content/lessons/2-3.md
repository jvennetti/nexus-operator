## Brief

The best prompts are conversations. This video shows one line that gets Claude asking you questions before it starts, so the first draft is closer to what you meant. Then you'll learn what to do after the first draft.

## Challenge

Have Claude ask first, then push for better.

1. Open a new chat and brief a real task. Add this line at the end:
```
Before you start, ask me three to five questions so you're 99% sure what I want.
```
2. Answer the questions in a few words each.
3. When the draft comes, give it three specific notes in one message. Not "make it better." Say exactly what's off.
4. Paste this and pick the best bits:
```
Now give me two more versions: one shorter and more direct, one written from the reader's point of view.
```
5. The next time Claude hedges or gives you a careful half-version, paste this and give it the context:
```
I understand the caveats. Do it fully. Here's the context:
```

Mark this done when you've used a draft that went through at least two rounds. [Most of the time Claude can. A real limit sounds different: it tells you the tool doesn't exist.]

## Takeaways

- Ask Claude to ask you questions first. Its questions show you what you forgot to say.
- Specific notes beat starting over. "The second paragraph is too formal" beats "try again."
- When Claude hedges, tell it you want it done and give it the context. Usually it can.
