## Brief

For most of history, if you wanted something done, you either did it yourself or found a person to do it. Those were the only two options. Hiring a person was slow, expensive and required you to know what you were doing well enough to judge their work.

That's the thing that changed. You now have, on your computer, something that can draft, research, plan, organize, summarize, write code, build documents and work inside your tools. It's fast, it's patient, and it never gets tired of being asked again.

Here's what it is **not**: it is not a search engine, and it is not a magic box. Claude is closer to a very capable new colleague who started this morning and knows nothing about you, your work, or what "good" looks like to you. Everything it produces is shaped by what you tell it.

That means the skill isn't technical. The skill is **directing**. Knowing what you want, saying it clearly, giving the context that makes it possible, and pushing back until it's right. That's the whole course.

People who struggle with AI treat it like a vending machine: put a vague request in, get a vague answer out, conclude it doesn't work. People who win with it treat it like a colleague they're onboarding. This module is about becoming the second kind of person, quickly.

## Task

Make your Operator List. This list follows you through the entire course.

1. **Think about your last week.** Not your job description. The actual tasks. The email you put off. The document you rebuilt from scratch. The research you did by opening fourteen tabs. The report you compile by hand every Monday.
2. **Write down three of them** that you would hand to a capable assistant if you had one. Be specific: not "emails" but "replying to the client who asked for a timeline on Thursday."
3. **Open a new chat and paste this,** with your three tasks filled in:
```
Here are three tasks from my week that I'd hand to a capable assistant if I had one:

1. [task one]
2. [task two]
3. [task three]

For each one, tell me honestly: could you do it today, partly, or not yet? And for each, what would you need from me (information, files, access) to do it well? Be concrete.
```
4. **Read the answer carefully.** Notice that for most tasks the answer is "yes, if you give me X." That X is what the rest of this course teaches you to provide.
5. **Save your list.** Rename the chat "Operator List." You'll add to it as you go.

## Takeaways

- Claude is a capable colleague who started this morning and knows nothing about you. What it produces is shaped by what you tell it.
- The skill is directing, not technology: knowing what you want, saying it clearly, providing context, and pushing back.
- Your Operator List is the real work you intend to hand off. Everything in this course is practiced against it.
