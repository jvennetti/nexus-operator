## Brief

Claude is like a very capable colleague who started this morning and knows nothing about you. This video is from Anthropic's AI Fluency course. It's about the first decision you make with any AI: which work to hand over, and which to keep.

## Challenge

Decide what you'd hand off.

1. Think about your last week. The actual tasks, not your job title. Things like the weekly report, the follow-up emails after meetings, the research before a call.
2. Write down three of them you'd give to a capable assistant. Be specific: not "emails," but "replying to the client who asked for a timeline."
3. Next to each one, write **automate** (Claude does it, you check it), **augment** (you do it together), or **keep** (you do it yourself).
4. Open a new chat and paste this with your list filled in:
```
Here are three tasks from my week that I'd hand to a capable assistant, with how I think each should be handled:

1. [task one]: [automate / augment / keep]
2. [task two]: [automate / augment / keep]
3. [task three]: [automate / augment / keep]

For each one: could you do it today, partly, or not yet? What would you need from me (information, files, access) to do it well? And do you agree with my tag?
```
5. Rename the chat "My tasks." You'll come back to it.

Mark this done when Claude has told you what it needs for each task.

## Takeaways

- Claude starts from zero. What it produces depends on what you tell it.
- Handing work off starts with knowing your own work: what's tedious, where you're unsure, what needs your judgment.
- Most "can you do this?" answers are "yes, if you give me X." This course teaches you to give it X.
