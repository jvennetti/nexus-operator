## Brief

For most of history, if you wanted something done, you did it yourself or you found a person to do it. Those were the only two options, and the second one was slow, expensive, and needed you to know the work well enough to judge it.

That's what changed. You now have, on your computer, something that can draft, research, plan, summarize, build documents and work inside your tools. It's fast, it's patient, and it never minds being asked again.

Here's what it is **not**: a search engine, or a magic box. Claude is closer to a very capable colleague who started this morning and knows nothing about you, your work, or what "good" means to you. Everything it produces is shaped by what you tell it.

People who struggle with AI treat it like a vending machine: vague request in, vague answer out, "it doesn't work." People who win with it treat it like a colleague they're onboarding. The video below is about the first decision an onboarding manager makes: which work to hand over, and which to keep.

## Challenge

Make your Operator List. This list follows you through the entire course.

1. **Think about your last week.** Not your job description. The actual tasks. The email you put off. The document you rebuilt from scratch. The research you did by opening fourteen tabs. The report you compile by hand every Monday.
2. **Write down three of them** you would hand to a capable assistant if you had one. Be specific: not "emails" but "replying to the client who asked for a timeline on Thursday." Make at least one of them something that isn't a chore: a decision you were unsure about, or a question you couldn't answer without digging.
3. **Tag each one** the way the video did: **automate** (Claude does it, you check it), **augment** (you and Claude work on it together), or **keep** (you do it yourself, and write one line on why).
4. **Open a new chat and paste this,** with your three tasks and tags filled in:
```
Here are three tasks from my week that I'd hand to a capable assistant if I had one, with how I think each should be handled:

1. [task one] — [automate / augment / keep]
2. [task two] — [automate / augment / keep]
3. [task three] — [automate / augment / keep]

For each one, tell me honestly: could you do it today, partly, or not yet? What would you need from me (information, files, access) to do it well? And do you agree with my tag, or would you change it? Be concrete.
```
5. **Read the answer carefully.** For most tasks it says "yes, if you give me X." That X is what the rest of this course teaches you to provide. Note the one place the answer surprised you.
6. **Save your list.** Rename the chat "Operator List." You'll add to it as you go.

## Takeaways

- Claude is a capable colleague who started this morning and knows nothing about you. What it produces is shaped by what you tell it.
- Delegation starts with knowing your own work: what's tedious, where you're unsure, what needs facts you don't have, and what needs your judgment.
- Your Operator List is the real work you intend to hand off. Everything in this course is practiced against it.
