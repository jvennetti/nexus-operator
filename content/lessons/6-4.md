## Brief

Things operators learn from use that no lesson teaches. Pick the ones that fit you.

**Speaking beats typing.** Voice dictation (your computer's built-in one, or a dedicated tool) makes briefs three times longer and three times better, because you say the context you'd never bother typing. Try it for a day.

**Keep a prompts file.** When a prompt works well, save it in your workspace. "The client update prompt." "The critique prompt." Next time, paste and adjust. Over a few months this becomes your personal operating manual.

**Ask it what it needs.** When you're not sure what to give Claude, ask: "What would you need from me to do this really well?" It will tell you, specifically.

**Ask it to be harsh.** "Be a brutal editor." "Tell me the three biggest weaknesses in this plan." "Argue the opposite." Claude defaults to agreeable. Ask for the opposite when you need it.

**Name the role.** "You are a senior financial analyst reviewing this." Changes the frame, the vocabulary, and what it notices.

**Use it to learn, not just to do.** "Explain what you just did as if I'm going to do it myself next time." You'll stop needing Claude for some things, which is the point.

**Rename your chats, archive your folders.** Future you will search for it.

**When in doubt, start over.** Fresh chat, one clean message, everything it needs. More problems are solved by this than by any clever technique.

**Tell it what you want.** When it hedges, hesitates, or gives you the safe version: "I want you to do it. Fully. Here's what you need." You're the director. Direct.

## Challenge

Adopt three habits, for real.

1. **Pick three** from the list above that you don't already do.
2. **Use each one today** on something real. Not as a test, on actual work.
3. **Create your prompts file.** In your workspace: "Create PROMPTS.md. Add the three best prompts I've used in this course, each with a one-line note on when to use it." Add to it any time a prompt works well.
4. **Write the ones that stuck into CLAUDE.md** or your Project instructions, so they're standing orders rather than things you remember to do.
5. **Add your own tip.** In the notes box below: one thing you've learned about working with Claude that wasn't in this course. You have one by now.

## Takeaways

- Speak your briefs. Save prompts that work. Ask Claude what it needs and ask it to be harsh.
- When stuck, start over with one clean message. It solves more problems than any trick.
- You're the director. When Claude hedges, tell it what you want. Fully.
