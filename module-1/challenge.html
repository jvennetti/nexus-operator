What We're Going to Do

The Handoff. Somewhere in your week there is work that doesn't need you. It needs doing, and it needs doing well, but it doesn't need your judgment or your taste. Until now there was nobody to give it to, so it stayed on your plate. That changed with this module.

In this mission you'll pick five pieces of that work and write them down in a way that makes them handable. Not "emails" or "reports," but the actual thing, with a clear picture of what finished looks like. By the end you'll have a one-page document that the rest of this school builds on, because every lesson from here takes one of those five and makes it real.

How We'll Do It
  1. 1
    Open your "My tasks" chat. You already have three tasks in it. Find two more by looking at last week: your calendar, your sent mail, the thing you did on Sunday night so Monday wouldn't hurt. Add them until you have five.
  2. 2
    For each task, write one sentence describing what "done" looks like. This is the part most people skip, and it's why their first attempts with Claude disappoint them. If you can't say what done looks like, Claude can't aim for it either. Tip: if a task won't fit in one sentence, it's probably two tasks. Split it.
  3. 3
    Now ask Claude what it would need from you. Paste:
    For each of my five tasks, tell me what you'd need to know, see, or have access to in order to do it well. One line per task. Then tell me which one you'd start with and why.
    Read the answer slowly. The useful part is anything you hadn't thought of: a file it would need, a decision it can't make for you, a piece of context only you have.
  4. 4
    Turn it into a document you'll keep. Paste:
    Turn this into a one-page document called My Tasks: the five tasks, what "done" looks like for each, what you'd need from me, and which one costs me the most time per week.
  5. 5
    Save it somewhere permanent. In Module 2 it goes into your project, where Claude will read it every time without you pasting it.
You're Done When

You're done when My Tasks is saved and you could hand any one of those five tasks to a new assistant tomorrow using only what's on the page.

Not everyone's week runs on a job. Meals, a budget, a club, a hobby, a job hunt: all of it is work with inputs and outputs, and all of it counts here.
